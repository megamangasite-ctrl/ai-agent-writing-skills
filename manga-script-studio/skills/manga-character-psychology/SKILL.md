# Character psychology

Work from a particular relationship and decision in the brief or draft. “Wants to belong” becomes writable when the lead holds the door for every team member and lets it close before entering herself.

1. State what the character wants from the other person **today**, what transaction they think will secure it, and what asking directly might expose. Give the habit a past or present benefit. Draw it working once before the price comes due.
2. Give the counterpart a separate want and a reasonable reason to refuse. Decide what each knows about the other at the start. If their misunderstanding could vanish in one sentence, give that sentence an interpersonal cost or let them say it and face the harder problem.
3. Pressure the same habit twice. On the first occasion it buys something. On the second it hurts something the lead values. In the third encounter let the lead repeat, revise or abandon it. A refusal to change can be a meaningful ending if its cost lands on the page.
4. Script the pivotal decision as two available actions with different losses. Draw the action taken and the next person's response. Remove the backstory paragraph and check that the reader can still see why this character acted.

**Deliver** a brief desire/habit/cost card for the lead and counterpart, then a three-panel acting beat for the difficult decision. In a revision, cite the panels that currently contradict the intended motive. Preserve deliberate contradictions that show a person behaving differently with different people.
