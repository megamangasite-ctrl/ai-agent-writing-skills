# Genre adaptation

Identify the beat that must survive a change of genre, the intended reader, and the pleasure promised by the brief. Audience labels can inform voice and access when specified. Ask what the audience came to experience before choosing conventions.

1. Lock the relationship, choice and cost. Pick a primary pleasure for the immediate scene, such as deciphering a clue, anticipating contact, matching a rival's move, or dreading the next ordinary sound.
2. Translate that pleasure into decisions about who knows what, when the page releases it, what the body does, and what qualifies as a fair payoff. For a mystery, plant a clue the reader can access before the answer. For romance, allow a risky bid and a legible response. For sports, make the relevant rule and counterplay visible before the deciding action.
3. Change the event enough to earn the new form. For the same core of “return a key and risk ending a relationship,” a romance can hold on the receiver leaving their palm open. A suspense version can show the key already turning on the far side of the door. Write the downstream consequence of that new information.
4. For a hybrid, assign each genre a job at the transition. A joke can lower a guard so a horror detail passes unnoticed. Check that the later danger preserves the earlier relationship and that the reader got the pleasure promised by the setup.

**Deliver** a short account of what survives, the changed scene in drawable beats, and a one-line genre contract pointing to the exact delivery panel. Flag any existing scene that now plants the wrong expectation. For whole arcs, pass the altered reveal and consequence to [arc planning](../manga-arc-planning/SKILL.md).
