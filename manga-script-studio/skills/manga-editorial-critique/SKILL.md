# Editorial critique

Record what is locked, what can change and what the creator wants back. Read the pages once without notes, synopsis, or explanatory annotations when possible. Record where attention rose, where an inference broke and what image lingered. Label these as one editorial reading.

1. Locate each material observation by page/panel or scroll beat. Separate the observable fact, the likely reader effect, and taste. “P3.4 names the winner before the turn” gives a repairable observation. “Add energy” gives an artist little to act on.
2. Identify one quality worth protecting. Rank the top problems by damage to that quality or to the brief. Repair cause and spatial clarity before polishing a line that may move. Name the strongest alternative interpretation where a quiet or ambiguous beat could already be working.
3. For the first repair, state the smallest viable change, a likely side effect, and a reread test. When a rewrite was requested, deliver the changed panels with neighboring context. When a critique was requested, make patch suggestions easy for the creator to accept or decline.
4. For a jury-style read, apply the [craft rubric](../../rubrics/artistic-craft.md). For discovery, use the [discovery rubric](../../rubrics/reader-discovery.md) separately. Cite the work under each judgment. Mark unavailable art, reader data, or rules as unknown. Preserve dissent instead of manufacturing panel consensus or jury predictions.

**Deliver** a short editor's letter containing the protected strength, ranked findings, selected change and test. Reread the actual patched sequence before claiming improvement. The [letter template](../../templates/editorial-letter.md) keeps observation and inference separate.
