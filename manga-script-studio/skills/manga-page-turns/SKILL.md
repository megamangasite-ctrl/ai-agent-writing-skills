# Pacing and page turns

Make the reader's visible field explicit. With print, record story-page sides and facing pairs, including covers, inserts or blank pages that change them. With a single-page reader, specify the advance gesture and any spread setting. With scroll, use ordered beats and the target viewport. A page number alone cannot certify a concealed reveal.

1. Identify the question at the end of the setup, the first image that could answer it, and the reaction that makes the answer matter. List revealing silhouettes, balloons, large lettering and captions as well as the principal image.
2. Draw a tiny visibility map. An example print map with right binding and a first story page alone on the left is `[P1] / [P2 right, P3 left] / [P4 right, P5 left]`. The slash marks a physical turn. P2 to P3 shares a view. For a hidden answer at P4, stage the unresolved last beat at P3 and keep P2–P3 visually clean of the answer.
3. Assign rhythm by attention. Tight repeated frames can stretch a breath. A dense scene can also read slowly through detail. Choose which information the eye will encounter first and what merits a second look. Use panel size, repetition, whitespace and balloon position according to that job.
4. Check in reading order **and** as a whole visible spread. If the neighboring page or a teaser leaks the answer, move the answer, mask the identifying feature, or use suspense about a consequence the reader already sees. Recheck after any page insertion.
5. For scroll, rebuild the interruption with staging and a tested reveal distance. Check short and tall target screens and variable text size where relevant. If concealment cannot be guaranteed, make early partial visibility create anticipation. For single-page mode, test the last panel, the tap and the first image after it.

**Deliver** before/after visibility, the final setup panel, first answer, reaction, and any layout assumption still awaiting thumbnails.
