# Arc planning

Record the page budget or episode budget, the opening imbalance, the last decision, and what survives it. If the creator has a fixed ending, work back to the earliest fact a reader needs for that ending to feel possible.

1. Build a chain of attempts. For each, name an actor, a tactic, resistance, and the changed situation that forces the next tactic. Trade any “and then” link for a consequence or cut the redundant beat. A quiet shift in permission, trust or knowledge can carry a reversal.
2. Find the point at which the opening tactic stops working. Ask what it cost the lead to find that out and which easy option has now closed. The final choice should use knowledge, skill, or willingness the opening version of the lead lacked.
3. Allocate contiguous page ranges or scroll units. Reserve space for a reaction **after** the largest turn. Give a complicated action room to become legible. Collapse travel, exposition, or a second example of the same point before cutting the aftermath.
4. Map plants to their returns. Check props, wounds, information, elapsed time and who witnessed each event across the chain. For a longer arc, track the changed relationship or rule that the next chapter inherits. For a short, mark the main dramatic question settled on-page.

**Deliver** a beat map with ranges that add up, one reason each beat causes the next, and the choice that resolves or deliberately redefines the opening question. If the budget cannot serve the cast and ending, propose a specific merge or cut and say what it preserves. Use the [beat and turn map](../../templates/beat-and-turn-map.md).
