# Emotional payoff

Describe the shift a reader may feel in concrete terms. “Proud of the runner for slowing down” gives more direction than “inspiring.” Locate the earlier evidence that makes that response available.

1. Set up an ordinary action, object, rhythm or phrase that matters before the ending. Give it a practical use so its return can surprise. A hand that always straightens a crooked picture can later steady it while somebody else signs the margin.
2. Give that behavior a second meaning through who performs it, what it costs or who now gets to respond. Let the opposing person retain agency. If the payoff depends on a late confession, coincidence or flashback, test an earlier visible plant for the information it introduces.
3. Separate the event that resolves the plot from the beat that lets the reader feel its consequence. Try a reaction from someone normally outside the frame, an ordinary task performed differently, or a silence whose length the artist can draw. Grief is one possible register alongside relief, embarrassment, dread and delight.
4. Remove the last explanatory line. Compare the last image with and without it. Keep the line only when the voice adds something the echo cannot carry. Check whether an earlier reader could identify the plant on reread and whether the ending still changes something for the character.

**Deliver** the plant and return with page references, the choice and cost between them, and the revised final beat. Offer a restrained alternate when the creator is choosing the emotional register. Use the [craft rubric](../../rubrics/artistic-craft.md) to distinguish a legible surprise from a felt consequence.
