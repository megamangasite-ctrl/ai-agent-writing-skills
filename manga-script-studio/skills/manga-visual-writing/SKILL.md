# Visual writing

Use the supplied display direction, page map, character identifiers and visual constraints. Establish who is where, what is reachable, and the direction of travel before a critical reversal. Preserve screen direction or give the reader a clear reorienting view.

1. Number pages and panels in reading order. Give each panel one dominant readable instant. Several people can do things in the same instant. Split “opens the case, reads the card and pockets it” wherever the reader needs a distinct state or reaction.
2. Turn an internal verb into evidence the artist can select. For “regrets signing,” try a pen cap crushed flat in the signer's other hand while the signed page slides away. Describe the indispensable relationship of hand, page and departing person. Leave lens, angle and decoration open when they carry no story information.
3. Establish scale, silhouette, value or texture for objects that must be distinguishable in black and white. Give crowds and places one economical anchor. Save detail for a clue, a shift in acting, or a place the reader has reason to revisit.
4. Mark dialogue, captions, sound effects (SFX) and readable in-world text separately. Number visible lettering in intended reading order and mark off-panel speech. Reserve space for it. An artist note stays outside the lettering. A silent panel can still contain a written sign, so honor any stricter wordless brief explicitly.
5. Do a silent pass for physical continuity, then add the lettering back and trace the eye path. Flag an unresolved spatial problem instead of asking the artist to convey incompatible actions.

**Deliver** the artist-facing pages using the [script-page template](../../templates/script-page.md). Include a single setup note for recurring wardrobe, geography or props. Point to the [clean six-page example](../../examples/the-back-half.md) when a collaborator needs the notation.
