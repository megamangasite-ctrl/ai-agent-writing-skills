# Brief and hook worksheet

Duplicate the fields you need. Separate decisions the creator already made from choices the agent may explore.

| Brief | Working entry |
| --- | --- |
| Deliverable and stop point | [logline, outline, script, critique, preview or dossier] |
| Reader and occasion | [who picks it up and what makes them curious] |
| Length, language and medium | [story page/beat count, language, print/tap/scroll] |
| Reading geometry | [direction, start side and visible pairs, or target viewport] |
| Emotional promise | [what experience the story offers and to whom] |
| Fixed facts and protected beat | [voice, ages, relationship, setting, ending or line] |
| Creative latitude | [what may change] |
| Destination or restrictions | [edition and official rules if applicable, AI use, prior publication, spoiler line] |
| Reversible assumptions | [choice and what evidence could change it] |

### Hook card

- **Situation in one breath** — [person] tries [specific action] before/because [pressure], even though [cost].
- **First visible discrepancy** — [what the eye expects] / [the detail that breaks the expectation].
- **Immediate question** — [what a stranger could ask after that panel].
- **Proof scene** — [the later action the premise makes possible and its effect].
- **Second reading** — [a detail whose meaning changes once the ending is known].
- **Strongest alternative** — [different engine or opening] / [what it gives up].

**Worked opening.** A new head performer in a two-person stage horse finally gets a face in the annual photo. Her old socks are visible beneath the tail in every picture on the wall. The contradiction can be drawn before the performers explain a thing.
