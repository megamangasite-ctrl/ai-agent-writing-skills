### P1 · The socks

The front performer changes in each print. The rear ankles stay the same. That makes Nell's three years of waiting visible without a flashback. “The socks” answers Bo's question and gives a joke to the reader who spotted them first. Nell trying to straighten an ear introduces her wish for this year's picture to come out right. Keep the socks large enough to compare in a printed thumbnail.

### P2 · A request that fits inside the costume

Bo asks Nell to wave for her. It is a modest favor between people doing a job. She could have said “I want to be seen too,” but that would give her knowledge of a grand gesture Nell hasn't imagined. The line about the oven mitt gives Bo's mother a particular sense of humor. Its pale silhouette also solves a later staging problem in monochrome. P2.4 locates both bodies before the story changes their arrangement.

### P3 · A choice the reader can interrupt

The offer at P3.4 matters because P3.2 has already given Nell exactly the attractive photograph she wanted. The mitt at P3.3 reminds her of a promise she could fulfill with one easy wave. Instead she reaches backward. In an earlier possible staging, Bo's face appeared above the cloth on P3.4. That would spend her decision on the setup page. The empty palm makes the physical turn do the work.

### P4 · Let the prop fail

The two performers have to ruin a good horse to make this picture. The slack cloth gives the reader a measure of the loss. Both still hold the prop, so Bo has visibly helped break the pose. A drawing of Nell dragging her out by the wrist would give the same plot outcome a different meaning. Keep the two faces legible at a glance. On the facing P5, keep the flash compact so it does not steal the first look from P4.

### P5 · The answer belongs to Bo

Bo can now return her mother's wave herself. Her “Leave it” interrupts a task Nell has been doing since P1.3. A discarded line, “We should both be in the picture,” would have repeated the work of P4 and left Bo with less personality. The flash closes the chance to tidy the pose.

### P6 · A second act of authorship

The new picture could have ended the story with a shared victory. Signing an *old* one lets Bo do something Nell couldn't do for herself. Nell holds the print still. The arrow points to a sock, and the first two panels now read differently. Keep the name off the displayed new photo so the last panel identifies Nell's years at the back.

### A preview that leaves the turn intact

**Cut** — P1.1–P1.2 in reading order. Keep the tail feet and both balloons. End before Nell picks up the head at P1.3. Hold P3.4–P6 and the new photo out of all preview collateral.

**Caption** — Three years in the cast photo. You can spot Nell if you know which socks to look for.

**Alt text** — “Which one's you?” Bo points to three photos of a stage horse, each with striped socks beneath the tail. She holds a photo by Nell's matching ankle. “The socks,” Nell says.

**Comparison copy** — Everyone remembers the front of the stage horse. Bo wants to know who was in the back.

Both captions use the same two panels. Compare whether a newcomer can retell the premise and name someone they'd send it to. No reader test or public release is implied. For a contest that restricts previews, keep the cut private until the edition's conditions are checked.
