# Reader discovery · excerpt assessment

Evaluate a particular cut, caption, display size and audience. A value describes the quality of the invitation. No score corresponds to a probability of going viral. Use **U** for conditions you cannot inspect.

| Axis | 0 · revise the premise of the cut | 1 · needs work | 2 · usable | 3 · precise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry for a stranger | Requires a synopsis or fandom fact | Lead or question is hard to infer | Reader can locate a person and a desire or discrepancy | The image supplies both quickly and rewards a second glance |
| Reason to pass it along | No identifiable pleasure or recipient | Generic excitement is the only reason | A particular reader might send it for recognition, wit or curiosity | A sender could add a personal sentence tied to an exact detail |
| Fidelity and spoiler control | Copy misrepresents events or gives away the protected answer | Accurate copy leans on an inference the story does not deliver | The crop promises a real scene and respects the boundary | The excerpt delivers a small payoff and leaves the story's larger choice intact |
| Legibility and access | Critical detail or text disappears at target size | Crop, order or alt text obscures the point | Sequence, lettering and visual description communicate the intended detail | The same essential inference survives a small display and an appropriate text description |

**Decision.** Replace or recut a misleading or spoiling excerpt regardless of its other scores. If print-size text cannot be inspected, supply an exact crop specification and tag display legibility U. For a consequential ambiguity, ask a cold reader what happened and what they expect next. Record observed answers separately from your editorial prediction.

**A test that can teach you something.** Change only the caption while keeping the image and audience comparable. Record unique exposed readers and unique readers who open the story where that data is available. The open rate uses exposed readers as its denominator. Pair it with a check that starters actually reach the promised scene. If comparable exposure cannot be established, report the counts and the uncertainty.
